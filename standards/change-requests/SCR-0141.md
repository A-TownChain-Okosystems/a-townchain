## Proposed Change

Authorize a controlled architecture reconciliation to determine and document the normative relationship between:

1. the seven independent product/technology pillars;
2. the system-of-systems architecture and its responsibility domains/layers;
3. normative repository classification and dependency constraints; and
4. cross-cutting control-plane responsibilities and interface contracts.

The reconciliation must produce an explicit, approved authority model and a source-to-source compatibility matrix. It must not silently select one existing document as superior, change normative meaning by implication, or treat this SCR itself as an approval.

**No normative architecture decision is made by this proposal.** Owner decision and the applicable review/approval process under ATC-STD-000 are required before standards, registries, validators or implementation mappings are changed.

## Motivation

Current authoritative sources use overlapping layer, component, product and dependency terminology with materially different scopes and dependency semantics. In particular, AD-026 explicitly records a sequential build/dependency hierarchy; ATC-STD-202 incorporates AD-026 into repository classification and dependency direction; ATC-ARCH-001 defines a six-core-technology KAI-OS architecture; ATC-STD-204 enforces declared dependency and interface rules; and the Ecosystem architecture describes L0–L7 as responsibility domains with X as a cross-cutting control plane.

The project product architecture separately identifies seven independent technology pillars. That product-architecture constraint is not, by itself, evidence that an approved normative standard or Owner decision has been amended. The conflict must therefore be resolved through the applicable governance process rather than by silently reinterpreting or bypassing existing approvals.

## 3. Required Architecture Invariants and Boundaries

The reconciliation must evaluate and preserve the following project constraints unless the Owner explicitly decides otherwise through the applicable governance process:

1. The product architecture identifies seven independent technology pillars:
   - ATCLang
   - ShivaCore Kernel
   - Aurora AI
   - A-TownChain Blockchain
   - ATC-VM
   - GlobusOS
   - Genesis Engine
2. The seven pillars are not to be presented as one universal linear runtime chain in product-facing architecture.
3. Aurora AI and Genesis Engine are not to be described as downstream products of GlobusOS.
4. A-TownChain Blockchain is not subordinate to ATCLang as a product or authority domain.
5. Integration is achieved through explicit interfaces, contracts, authorization boundaries and evidence—not by relocating component ownership.
6. Each implementation capability retains exactly one canonical implementation SSOT, as established by applicable repository ownership records.
7. Product taxonomy, system responsibility domains, repository governance, runtime/operational flows and cross-cutting controls are distinct analytical models unless an approved specification explicitly defines their relationship.
8. ATC-STD-204 dependency and interface controls remain binding. This SCR does not authorize implicit dependencies or weaken security, CI or governance gates.
9. Architecture descriptions are not implementation or verification evidence.

**Normative caveat:** These are reconciliation constraints requested for the product architecture. They do not amend or override AD-026, ATC-STD-202, ATC-ARCH-001 or ATC-STD-204 before the Owner decision and required approvals.

## 4. Reconciliation Work Packages

### 4.1 Establish source authority and scope

For each source, record:
- document type and normative/informative status;
- approval authority, approval evidence and lifecycle state;
- scope and entities governed;
- definitions of layer, pillar, component, repository, dependency and runtime order;
- requirements that are binding versus explanatory diagrams/examples;
- supersession, compatibility and conflict-resolution rules.

Determine whether Ecosystem ARCHITECTURE.md is informative, a normative architecture contract, or a controlled architecture source referenced by standards. Do not infer normative authority from filename or ACTIVE status alone.

### 4.2 Complete the clause-level conflict matrix

The matrix in §2 is the initial clause-level baseline. During formal review it must be expanded with:
- exact paragraph/requirement identifiers where available;
- exact source commit and blob references;
- source approval artifacts and decision authority;
- affected standards, repositories, registry records and validator rules;
- compatibility impact and proposed disposition;
- approving authority and review outcome.

### 4.3 Separate architecture taxonomies

Evaluate whether the normative model should explicitly distinguish:
- **Product/Pillar Taxonomy:** independent products and technology systems;
- **System Responsibility Domains:** system/network, persistence, consensus, deterministic execution, scaling, protocol/economic services, intelligence and user experience;
- **Repository Governance Taxonomy:** repository type, maturity, canonical ownership and security classification;
- **Runtime/Operational Flows:** actual execution and data-flow sequences, defined only where required by a concrete contract;
- **Cross-Cutting Control Plane:** identity, capability, authorization, policy, governance, cryptography, audit, evidence and compatibility.

A mapping between these taxonomies may be many-to-many. It must not be used to manufacture product dependencies or change SSOT ownership.

### 4.4 Define dependency semantics

Using ATC-STD-204 as a required compatibility input, distinguish:
- compile-time dependency;
- runtime/service dependency;
- protocol or data-contract dependency;
- optional integration;
- governance/specification reference;
- evidence/catalog reference;
- cross-cutting authorization or policy enforcement.

For each actual declared dependency specify provider, consumer, interface/version, direction, authority owner, failure behavior and validation evidence. A layer label alone is not proof of a runtime dependency, but an existing normative layer-direction rule remains binding until formally changed.

### 4.5 Assess existing consumers and migration

Before any normative change:
- enumerate standards, repository manifests, dependency registries, schemas, validators, CI workflows, generated views, architecture diagrams and product documents that consume AD-026 or affected layer identifiers;
- identify hard-coded assumptions and compatibility risks;
- define migration and deprecation behavior;
- preserve historical identifiers and decision records;
- prevent partial migrations in which standards, registries and validators disagree.

## 5. Decision Options for Owner Review

The reconciliation team should evaluate, not preselect, at least these options:

**Option A — Separate taxonomies (preferred for evaluation, not approved):** retain product pillars, system responsibility domains, repository classification and runtime flows as distinct models, connected by explicit mapping and interface contracts. Explicitly define whether AD-026's sequence governs build readiness, runtime dependency, repository dependency, or a combination. Reconcile ATC-STD-202 and ATC-ARCH-001 so their normative scope is unambiguous.

**Option B — Retain AD-026 as the sole normative layer hierarchy:** keep the existing L0–L7 directionality and sequential build semantics, but explicitly limit the hierarchy to the domain it governs; define product pillars and cross-cutting controls separately and prevent layer numbering from being interpreted as a universal product-runtime chain.

**Option C — Revise the normative architecture model:** replace or materially revise the current hierarchy through a versioned breaking-change process, with complete consumer inventory, migration plan, compatibility controls and explicit Owner approval.

No option may be implemented by editing only the Ecosystem master architecture or only the strategy document. The selected option must be reflected consistently in every affected normative source and its enforcement tooling.
